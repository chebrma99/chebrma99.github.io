Engagement Optimization in Large-Scale Video Platforms: A Systems Review of Metrics, Ranking, Experimentation, Latency, and Feedback Loops

Manoja Chebrolu

\n

Abstract

\n

Video engagement systems are central to streaming, short-form social media, and commerce platforms, yet engagement remains difficult to optimize because platforms observe proxy signals rather than satisfaction directly. This paper presents a systems review of large-scale video engagement, synthesizing work on engagement metrics, multi-stage recommendation architecture, online experimentation, latency-sensitive serving, personalization feedback loops, and commerce-specific video usefulness. The review argues that engagement is an emergent property of the full platform pipeline: logs define measurable behavior, retrieval determines what can be ranked, ranking and re-ranking impose objective trade-offs, experiments decide which changes are trusted, infrastructure determines whether recommendations are experienced responsively, and personalization feedback loops shape future exposure. Unlike model-centered surveys, this paper treats engagement optimization as a cross-layer systems problem. The review identifies five recurring findings: engagement proxies are necessary but incomplete; industrial video recommenders rely on multi-stage ranking pipelines; online controlled experiments remain essential for validating real user impact; latency and tail performance can reduce observed engagement even when ranking quality improves; and behavior-driven personalization creates feedback loops that require governance, explanation, and user-control mechanisms. The paper concludes with research challenges in long-term value measurement, latency-aware ranking, feedback-loop mitigation, transparency, and commerce-oriented video evaluation.
